Compliance

Built for the Minnesota Rules.

The AI Legal Associate is designed around the Minnesota Rules of Professional Conduct: a tool under your supervision, not a replacement for your judgment.

Rule 1.1: Competence

The agent works under your supervision, and you remain competent in the tools you use. Its outputs arrive with their verification verdicts attached, so you can review with full context, not blind trust.

Rule 1.6: Confidentiality

One agent per attorney, isolated per matter. Only task-relevant content is used for each step, your files are not used for training, and nothing leaves the workflow without your direction.

Rule 3.3: Candor

The citation gate exists for this rule: it refuses to invent, flags what it can't verify, and keeps unverified citations out of your drafts. Candor is designed in, not hoped for.

Rule 5.3: Supervision

You review and approve every output. Nothing auto-files, nothing auto-submits, and every action leaves an audit trail. The agent assists; you direct.

Transparency

Where Minnesota’s AI rules are going

Minnesota’s rules for AI in professional services are still moving. SF 4927, a bill before the Legislature on delivering professional services through AI directly to consumers, has not been enacted, and we do not claim compliance with any statute that has not taken effect.

What we build to today is the Minnesota Rules of Professional Conduct: you direct the work, you review every output, and you are the author. Every AI-assisted action leaves a documented audit trail.

Disclosure language for client engagement is ready for your review; you decide what to use.

The line we will not cross

Unauthorized practice of law

The agent drafts. The attorney reviews, decides, and submits. The attorney is the author.

No workflow auto-submits anything to a court or a client. Every draft carries SUBMIT: None until you approve it. The judgment is yours, and the design keeps it that way.

The gavel, and the judgment is yours

We design to the rules as written. We are not certified by any authority, and we don't claim to be. The final judgment under the rules is yours, and that's exactly how we want it.

Limits, stated plainly

What we do not have, said here rather than left for you to find

Limits are stated rather than left to be discovered. That is the same standard we apply to the associate itself.

Coverage is never complete

No research source covers everything, including ours. Where coverage is thin or a source is unavailable, that is flagged rather than filled in by inference.

Commercial editorial content is not included

The open and official layer of law is ours and is included. The editorial layer the large commercial services own, the headnotes, the key numbers, and the annotated codes, is not ours and no Merit subscription includes it. Where your firm holds such a subscription, it can be used alongside the associate, in your name and on your credential.

A treatment signal is not a citator

Where a case has been treated, that is labelled as a signal and never presented as a citator. If a question turns on treatment, it belongs in the source you subscribe to.

No firm-wide portfolio analysis

The analysis scope is your own environment. Analysis across attorneys is a separate, separately authorised capability with its own purpose and access rules, and it is not part of the standard service.

Certifications are not held

SOC 2 and ISO 27001 are not held and are not claimed. Independent penetration testing is performed before the first attorney is onboarded and at least annually after that. AI liability insurance and technology errors and omissions cover will be in place before any firm is delivered a seat.

Databases are the firm's own

A commercial research subscription is not included and is never resold. Where your firm holds one, it stays your firm's: connected only on your written authorisation, used in your own name, never read by automation, never copied, never retained beyond the session.
