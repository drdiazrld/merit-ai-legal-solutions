vs General AI

Commands, not prompts.

Tell it what to do. It does the work.

A general-purpose AI chatbot waits for you to manage it. The Associate takes plain-English commands through a multi-step workflow. The standalone citation gate is implemented, but the desk does not call it yet; ACC-01 wiring is in flight.

The commands

Say it like you'd say it to a paralegal

One plain-English command at a time. The same command does the same work every time. That's the point of a command.

The briefs work

  • "Draft a memo for the case"Writes the memo from the matter record: parties, facts, issues, evidence, your style.
  • "Draft a demand letter"The letter, in litigation format, with citations drawn from matter research for attorney review.
  • "Draft a motion"The motion, the notice, and the supporting authorities: one command.
  • "Prepare me for tomorrow's deposition"Decomposes into parallel workstreams: facts, research, evidence, weaknesses, opposing position, tasks, and questions. It merges them into one prep brief.

The matter work

  • "New matter: Johnson v. Smith, rear-end collision, 2026-03-14"Builds the matter record: parties, facts, chronology, issues, evidence, weaknesses, opposing position, next actions.
  • "Analyze the file /path/to/complaint.pdf"Extracts the allegations, classifies them, and logs the evidence in the matter.
  • "What's due?" or "What's due in 14 days?"Deadlines: business days, Minnesota holidays, and the +3 mail rule, calculated from your matter's dates.
  • "Add a task: file the response by 2026-09-01" and "Mark T1 done"Tasks in the matter record, tracked until you close them.
  • "Is 518.17 real?"A citation check against the 630-authority Minnesota and federal index; the standalone gate returns a verdict when it is run (desk wiring in flight under ACC-01).

The desk pipeline pulls the matter record, routes the work, drafts, and marks it READY_FOR_ATTORNEY_REVIEW. The standalone citation gate is not yet called by that path; ACC-01 wiring is in flight. Nothing auto-files. SUBMIT stays None until you approve.

How that differs

One command is a pipeline, not a chat turn

With a general-purpose chatbot, you are the project manager: you paste the context, restate the task, check the citations, and re-do it all next session. With the Associate, you are the reviewer. That's the whole difference.

1

It pulls the matter itself

Parties, facts, chronology, evidence, tasks: the living file in the Case Brain. You don't re-explain the case; you reference it.

2

It routes the work to the right model tier

Drafting, analysis, and deadline math route through the desk. A separate gate can check citations against the 630-authority index and return VERIFIED, NOT_RETRIEVABLE, or AMBIGUOUS, but the desk does not call that gate yet.

3

It leaves the work honest

Empty fields stay empty: no filler, no confident guessing. The draft comes back marked READY_FOR_ATTORNEY_REVIEW, and nothing leaves without you: SUBMIT is None until you approve.

4

The gate is separate today

The verification and adversarial checks exist, but they are not yet wired into every desk brief. ACC-01 is in flight.

Why it's easier

Fewer steps. Fewer decisions.

1

Order-of-magnitude fewer steps

One command replaces a dozen prompts. Fewer steps means fewer decisions about how to ask, what to paste, and what to check: the decisions that drain a day.

2

You remain the verifier today

The standalone citation guard can attach a verdict to an authority when it runs. Until ACC-01 wires it into the desk, attorneys must continue to verify draft citations.

3

It remembers the case

Per-attorney matter records, evidence logs, task lists, and your preferences, recorded under your review. No session starts from zero.

4

Built on the ethics rules

Rule 1.1, 1.6, 3.3, and 5.3 are the architecture: attorney-review gates, per-attorney isolation, and an audit trail on everything. No compliance briefing required.

5

It says no, honestly

Off-topic, non-Minnesota, or unverifiable? It declines instead of guessing confidently. You never wonder whether a clean answer is a real answer.

It's discipline: verification, refusal, organization, and your review on every deliverable. That's the whole difference, and it's the only one that matters for legal work.
