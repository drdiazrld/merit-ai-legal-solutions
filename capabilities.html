The review boundary

  1. Attorney inputInstructions, facts, records, and governing material.
  2. Merit preparationA bounded draft, organization, or proposed calculation.
  3. Attorney reviewCheck facts, sources, law, calculations, and form.
  4. Manual actionThe attorney files, sends, serves, or submits.

Five workflows and review steps

Legal research with sources

Attorney supplies
The attorney provides the question, jurisdiction, facts, and controlling records.
Merit prepares
Merit prepares a research draft with sources.
Attorney reviews or does
Open every source. Confirm its existence, currency, support, and application.

Draft preparation

Attorney supplies
The attorney provides the task, audience, records, position, and drafting instructions.
Merit prepares
Merit prepares a draft from the supplied records and instructions.
Attorney reviews or does
Check it against the record and law. Revise it and decide whether to use it.

Matter intake and organization

Attorney supplies
Intake information and matter records.
Merit prepares
Parties, dates, documents, and open questions.
Attorney reviews or does
Correct errors, supply missing facts, and maintain the official file.

Court-filing document preparation

Attorney supplies
Intended filing, court information, records, and approved form.
Merit prepares
A court-filing document for review.
Attorney reviews or does
Review, correct, sign when appropriate, and submit manually.

Proposed deadline calculations

Attorney supplies
The attorney provides the trigger, dates, rule or order, court, and known exceptions.
Merit prepares
Merit prepares a proposed calculation that identifies the inputs used.
Attorney reviews or does
Calculate the deadline independently. Check the rules, orders, holidays, service, and exceptions.

What these tests do and do not show

The public test evidence applies only to the components and paths described on the testing page. Attorneys must still calculate and verify proposed deadlines independently. Merit makes no broad deadline-accuracy claim.

Outside the current scope

The five workflows do not include discovery or billable-time records. Merit does not give legal advice, exercise professional judgment, file or serve documents, make final legal decisions, or replace firm personnel.

Merit may fit a firm that has time to supervise the work. It is not suitable where the attorney cannot independently review facts, law, citations, calculations, and the final document.
